## Search protocol

### Sources, queries, and cutoff

The audit began with the 23 BibTeX records and 11 PDFs present before this pass. It then used canonical publisher/proceedings records, Crossref, DBLP, arXiv/bioRxiv, PMC/Europe PMC, institutional repositories, OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar, official package documentation, and author/project repositories. Representative query families were:

- `random forest proximity`, `random forest dissimilarity`, `terminal node similarity`, and `co-leaf kernel`;
- `random forest kernel`, `random partition kernel`, `leaf incidence kernel`, `path kernel`, `RF-GAP`, and `PF-GAP`;
- those terms crossed with clustering, anomaly, imputation, matching, quantile, conformal, explanation, prototype, manifold, multi-view, time series, and graph neural network;
- foundational method families from CART/C4.5 through bagging/RF/Extra-Trees, boosting systems, oblique/projection forests, anomaly/distributional/causal forests, and Bayesian trees.

Backward references, forward citations, related-paper expansion, exact-title identity searches, and DOI-first deduplication were used. The cutoff is the snapshot date above. The final two targeted snowball passes added no new application theme; additions were below 5% of the final corpus, so the plan's stopping rule was met.

### Inclusion, exclusion, and version rules

A direct application must define, analyze, evaluate, or consume downstream a relation induced by a fitted forest/tree ensemble. A tree that only consumes an externally supplied distance is adjacent. Generic kernels, ordinary RF applications without proximity use, ecological uses of “forest proximity,” and duplicate preprint/final versions are excluded. Final bibliographic metadata supersedes a preprint; a lawful preprint/accepted manuscript may be the local full text and is explicitly related to the final record.

### Evidence tiers and verification

- **Tier A:** canonical/seminal evidence plus strong practical adoption, or exceptional age-adjusted influence plus adoption.
- **Tier B:** at least two strong signals among citations, adoption, historical influence, and direct project relevance.
- **Tier C:** emerging, specialized, or adjacent work retained for its relevance, without a broad-importance claim.
- **V3:** full text and canonical metadata inspected; **V2:** primary abstract/extended record and metadata inspected; **V1:** metadata/secondary evidence only, so substantive claims need verification.

### Citation snapshot and adoption rules

OpenAlex `cited_by_count` and Semantic Scholar `citationCount (influentialCitationCount)` are preserved separately and never summed. Per-year values use `raw / (2026 - canonical publication year + 1)`. DOI was matched first, then normalized title plus authors; duplicate preprint/final counts were not aggregated. OpenAlex changed keyless API accounting in 2026 and the shared keyless daily quota was exhausted during the application pass. Captured counts remain in the tables; `NA` is unavailable, never zero. Semantic Scholar resolved all final direct works except where explicitly shown. Index disagreements are evidence-quality warnings rather than values to “correct.”

Adoption is triangulated from maintained major-library support, official/author-linked implementations, package/repository activity, and paper evidence. GitHub stars are only a snapshot proxy. A repository is called open source only where an explicit license was verified.

- **CART:** OpenAlex fragments the book across a 2017 DOI reissue and multiple book-review-like records (`W1594031697`, 23,901; `W3085162807`, 21,050). S2 likewise returns several polluted records. The table uses the clean reissue record and a conservative closest-title S2 record; **do not sum them**.
- **ID3:** OpenAlex also has `W2149706766` with DOI alias `10.1023/A:1022643204877` and 14,871 citations. The table uses the canonical Springer DOI work `W4236137412`; do not sum alias records.
- **Bagging:** OpenAlex has alias record `W2912934387` (`10.1023/A:1018054314350`, 17,031) alongside the canonical `10.1007/BF00058655` record. The S2 record merges identity more aggressively, explaining part of the larger count.
- **LightGBM:** OA has a sparse final-proceedings record (`W2753094203`, title missing “LightGBM,” 124 citations) and a much more cited exact-title/HAL record (`W2768348081`, 9,487). The latter is reported, while canonical metadata remains the NeurIPS final.
- **CatBoost:** OA's exact-title work is the 2017 preprint and substantially trails S2's final NeurIPS identity. The canonical catalog year is 2018.
- **SPORF:** OA indexes the 2015 preprint with only 8 citations even though the final JMLR paper is 2020; S2 appears to merge the JMLR identity. Keep both counts as-is.
- **QRF:** OA also contains a low-count JMLR duplicate carrying a `10.5555` identifier; the stronger exact-title work `W1573647811` is reported.
- **Causal Forest:** OA canonical DOI work uses online year 2017; catalog year is the 2018 JASA issue. Separate preprint fragments exist and are not added.
- **GRF:** OA's DOI work has only 16 citations versus S2's 1,737. This is an obvious indexing/identity failure, not evidence of low influence; Tier A is supported by the canonical final paper, S2, and maintained official adoption.
- **BART:** OA separates final and repository/preprint records (the latter `W3099006712`, 970); the table reports only the final DOI work.

### Detailed application evidence

1. **`breiman2001randomforests` — Breiman, “Random Forests,” Machine Learning 45 (2001), 5–32. DOI `10.1023/A:1010933404324`.** Relation: the original supervised proximity is the fraction of trees in which two observations occupy the same terminal leaf; the paper also introduces unsupervised synthetic-reference forests and proximity-based MDS, imputation, outlier scores, and class prototypes. Reported evidence spans benchmark classification/regression and exploratory tools. Limitation: raw collision counts ignore OOB/in-bag roles, bootstrap multiplicity, and leaf mass; dense all-pairs storage is quadratic. Code: author-linked Berkeley RF software, no explicit license verified; third-party R `randomForest` is separately licensed. OA: lawful author/local copy exists, final publisher metadata retained. Citations: **OA NA / S2 119,262 (7,137)**. Relevance: canonical baseline every new proximity must define against. **V3**. Local full text: [RandomForest.pdf](../papers/RandomForest.pdf).

2. **`lin2006adaptive` — Lin and Jeon, “Random Forests and Adaptive Nearest Neighbors,” JASA 101 (2006), 578–590. DOI `10.1198/016214505000001230`.** Relation: rewrites forest prediction as an adaptive weighted-neighbor/potential-nearest-neighbor smoother, with co-leaf training points weighted by leaf occupancy across trees. Task: regression/classification theory and adaptive local smoothing. Evidence: theoretical characterization connects terminal-node size and locally selected variables to neighborhood geometry. Limitation: it is primarily a prediction-weight view, not a generally symmetric PSD pairwise kernel; assumptions do not cover every modern RF implementation. Code: not found. OA: no lawful open final located. Citations: **OA NA / S2 576 (41)**. Relevance: key bridge between prediction weights and proximity geometry. **V2**. Local full text: not held in this lawful-OA subset.

3. **`shi2006unsupervised` — Shi and Horvath, “Unsupervised Learning With Random Forest Predictors,” JCGS 15 (2006), 118–138. DOI `10.1198/106186006X94072`.** Relation: train a classifier to separate observed samples from a synthetic null, then convert tree co-membership/prediction behavior to dissimilarities for clustering. Evidence: simulations and biological data illustrate recovery of nonlinear/mixed-type structure. Limitations: geometry depends materially on how synthetic data are generated; deep leaves can make raw proximity sparse; labels from the real-vs-synthetic task do not equal downstream cluster truth. Code: not found. OA: no verified lawful open final. Citations: **OA NA / S2 528 (25)**. Relevance: canonical unsupervised-RF construction. **V2**. Local full text: not held in this lawful-OA subset.

4. **`scornet2016kernel` — Scornet, “Random Forests and Kernel Methods,” IEEE TIT 62 (2016), 1485–1500. DOI `10.1109/TIT.2016.2514489`; arXiv `1502.03836`.** Relation: KeRF turns forest leaf co-membership into a normalized kernel smoother, including leaf-mass weighting. Evidence: consistency/risk analysis for simplified forest models and empirical comparisons with RF. Limitations: strongest theory uses stylized forests; KeRF and deployed Breiman RF are not identical predictors. Code: not found. OA: arXiv. Citations: **OA NA / S2 277 (23)**. Relevance: primary formal RF-kernel theory and leaf-mass comparator. **V3**. Local full text: [Scornet2016RandomForestsKernels.pdf](../papers/Scornet2016RandomForestsKernels.pdf).

5. **`englund2012proximity` — Englund and Verikas, “A novel approach to estimate proximity in a random forest: An exploratory study,” ESWA 39 (2012), 13046–13050. DOI `10.1016/j.eswa.2012.05.094`.** Relation: a path/branch-based kernel scores how closely observations traverse a tree, rather than using only terminal-leaf collision, and averages over trees for SVM use. Reported evidence: classification experiments show advantages most clearly with small forests. Limitations: pairwise path processing and a scale parameter increase cost/tuning; gains narrow as tree count rises. Code: not found. OA: no verified lawful copy. Citations: **OA 34 / S2 37 (3)**. Relevance: closest non-leaf RF path-kernel comparison. **V2**. Local full text: not held in this lawful-OA subset.

6. **`davies2014randompartition` — Davies and Ghahramani, “The Random Forest Kernel and other kernels for big data from random partitions” (2014). arXiv `1402.4293`.** Relation: a random-partition kernel averages co-membership in a partition chosen at a randomized tree resolution/height; RF and fast-cluster instantiations yield explicit scalable approximations. Evidence: kernel likelihood, GP/SVM/kernel-PCA demonstrations on real datasets. Limitations: random-height coarse partitions differ from canonical leaf proximity; preprint only; no public implementation found. Code: not found. OA: arXiv. Citations: **OA 36 (canonical record; duplicate OA record 22 not summed) / S2 72 (8)**. Relevance: partition-kernel generalization beyond terminal leaves. **V3**. DOI `10.1109/TPAMI.2023.3263774`; arXiv `2201.12682`.** Relation: RF-GAP queries trees where an observation is OOB and weights in-bag co-leaf references by bootstrap multiplicity and leaf mass. Its directed weights exactly reconstruct the forest's OOB/test regression prediction or class vote. Evidence: proofs plus imputation, visualization, and outlier experiments. ### Screening exclusions and direction-of-relation checks

- **Distance-consuming rather than relation-inducing:** the base Proximity Forest, Similarity Forests, Random Similarity Forests, and Proximity Isolation Forest accept distances/similarities to construct splits. They are adjacent unless a post-fit relation such as PF-GAP is explicitly derived.
- **CLIQUE/conditional local importance:** the current TMLR/arXiv work (`bladen2024conditional`, arXiv `2411.08821`) is model-agnostic and does not use forest proximity. The starting arXiv ID `2402.18384` was unrelated and has been corrected in the bibliography.
- **Unverified identity:** no primary record was found for Zheng and Mazumdar, “A Kernel Approach to Random Forests.” The uncited `zheng2007pbk` entry was removed rather than repaired speculatively.
- **Misleading retained filename:** `RandomProjectionForests.pdf` is Lee, Yang, and Oh, “Fast and Accurate Head Pose Estimation via Random Projection Forests,” ICCV 2015, DOI `10.1109/ICCV.2015.227`. It is neither the Cannings–Samworth ensemble paper nor a proximity application.
- **Eligible but outside the final 49-work direct set:** the multiscale Zafari kernel, Sidak URF tutorial, Al-Husain cluster ensemble, Zhao fuzzy multiple-kernel clustering, Peerbhay invasive-plant mapping, Cao breast-histology transfer study, Packalen inventory imputation, Ishioka semi-supervised imputation, and Qiu PPI-site study were screened. They added no new theme or closer comparison and were left out under the stopping/priority rule.
- **Generic exclusions:** `missForest`, ordinary prediction-only RF papers, generic kernel/SVM papers, ecological uses of “forest proximity,” and duplicate preprint/final records are out of scope. Final/preprint citation counts are not summed.


### Project-positioned gap analysis

The statements in this subsection are **project inferences**, not claims made by every cited paper.

- The closest definitional pair is RF-GAP (`pang2023rfgap`) plus sparse leaf-incidence/SWLC (`aumon2026revisiting`): the former supplies prediction-faithful directed weights; the latter supplies a unifying sparse factorization and kernel/bilinear distinction.
- Lin–Jeon, Scornet, Olson–Wyner, generalized random forests, quantile forests, and AXIL show that tree ensembles can be read as adaptive training-instance smoothers. They do not jointly provide arbitrary forest-type support, OOB/bootstrap semantics, sparse leaf factors, and a common downstream application interface.
- Most application papers materialize dense, usually symmetric relations and validate one domain. Few separate inductive test-to-train extension from transductive train-to-train geometry, and even fewer audit leakage introduced by label-trained neighborhoods.
- Oblique/projection forests are well studied as predictors, but the downstream geometry of their induced neighborhoods is much less systematically compared than axis-aligned RF proximity.
- Open gaps include calibrated uncertainty for learned local weights, causal guarantees beyond balance/imputation experiments, controlled missingness benchmarks, sparse graph construction without transductive leakage, and evaluations where improved visual separation is not mistaken for structure preservation.

## Reproducibility and known limitations

- Canonical metadata was resolved DOI-first, then by exact normalized title/authors. Every final-table key is required to occur exactly once in `../../ObliqueProximitiesPaper/main.bib`.
